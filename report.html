Project Report

A one-page summary of the objectives, data sources, methods, and key findings of the Australia Environmental Monitoring project.

Overview

Objective

This project builds a cloud-native geospatial pipeline that monitors six key environmental signals across the Australian continent using freely available satellite data processed on Google Earth Engine. It delivers an interactive WebGIS, seven static maps, and a reproducible Python workflow that refreshes automatically when new data becomes available.

Data & Methodology

Fire — Burned Area

🔥 MODIS MCD64A1

Monthly global burned-area product combining 500 m MODIS surface-reflectance imagery with a burn-sensitive vegetation index and thermal anomalies.

  • Resolution: 500 m
  • Period: 2021
  • Band used: BurnDate
  • Aggregation: annual max composite
Fire — Active Detections

🔥 VIIRS SNPP (NASA LANCE)

Near-real-time active fire product from the Suomi NPP satellite, with 375 m spatial resolution and 2–4 daily overpasses.

  • Resolution: 375 m
  • Period: 8–30 October 2023
  • Band used: Bright_ti4 (Kelvin)
  • Classes: 5 temperature bins (300–380+ K)
Temperature

🌡️ MODIS MOD11A1

Daily land surface temperature from the Terra satellite, corrected for atmospheric and emissivity effects.

  • Resolution: 1 km
  • Period: 2019 (full year)
  • Band used: LST_Day_1km
  • Units converted from K to °C
Vegetation

🌿 MODIS MOD13A2

16-day composite Normalized Difference Vegetation Index — the standard measure of vegetation vigour and density.

  • Resolution: 1 km
  • Period: 2010–2021
  • Aggregation: monthly mean → annual mean
  • Scale factor applied: ×0.0001
Land Cover

🌍 MODIS MCD12C1

Yearly global land cover at climate-model resolution, using the IGBP classification scheme remapped to 8 aggregated classes.

  • Resolution: 500 m
  • Period: 2022
  • Band used: Majority_Land_Cover_Type_1
  • Remapped: IGBP 16 → 8 groups
Forest Change

🌲 Hansen Global Forest Change

Landsat-derived stand-replacement forest loss, updated annually. The most widely used dataset for tracking forest dynamics.

  • Resolution: 30 m
  • Period: 2020–2022
  • Band used: loss (masked by lossyear)
  • Exported as binary loss raster

Processing Pipeline

Workflow

From satellite to WebGIS in 6 steps

  1. Ingest — six Earth Engine ImageCollections are filtered by date and Australia's national boundary.
  2. Reduce — temporal aggregations (mean, max, count) produce one summary image per layer.
  3. Classify — continuous variables are converted into discrete classes for clear visualisation.
  4. Serve — each layer is registered as a Google Earth Engine tile endpoint (XYZ URL).
  5. Render — the tile URLs are consumed by a Leaflet-based WebGIS with toggleable layers and opacity control.
  6. Archive — every map is exported as a high-resolution PNG with a black background, and as a GeoTIFF to Google Drive.

Key Findings

🔥 Fire

Northern Australia dominates fire activity

Over 90% of VIIRS fire detections in October 2023 occurred in the tropical savannas of the Northern Territory and Queensland — a region that burns annually during the late dry season.

🌡️ Temperature

Deserts exceed 45 °C seasonally

Mean daytime LST in 2019 exceeded 45 °C across the Great Victoria and Gibson deserts, while coastal Tasmania and Victoria averaged below 20 °C.

🌿 Vegetation

Sharp contrast between coast and interior

Mean NDVI for 2010–2021 exceeded 0.6 along the east coast and southwest forests, but stayed below 0.15 across the central arid zone.

🌲 Forest

Forest loss concentrated in the east

Between 2020 and 2022, measurable forest loss occurred primarily in NSW and southeast Queensland, coinciding with the 2019–2020 bushfire recovery period and ongoing land-use change.

Reproducibility

Open science

Everything is scriptable and free

All layers are generated from freely available datasets via the Google Earth Engine Python API. The complete pipeline is contained in a Jupyter notebook plus three helper scripts. Tile URLs can be regenerated at any time by re-running generate_tile_urls.py, and the WebGIS can be redeployed to Vercel with a single command.

  • 🐍 Python 3.10+ with earthengine-api and geemap
  • 📓 Jupyter notebook for the analysis
  • 🌐 Static HTML/CSS/JS WebGIS served on Vercel
  • 📦 All inputs open-licensed: NASA, USGS, University of Maryland
