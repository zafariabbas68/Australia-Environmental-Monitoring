01

Overview

This project builds a cloud-native geospatial pipeline that monitors six key environmental signals across the Australian continent using freely available satellite data processed on Google Earth Engine. It delivers an interactive WebGIS, seven static map products, and a reproducible Python workflow.

03

Processing Pipeline

  1. 01

    Ingest

    Six Earth Engine ImageCollections are filtered by date and Australia's national boundary.

  2. 02

    Reduce

    Temporal aggregations — mean, max, count — produce one summary image per layer.

  3. 03

    Classify

    Continuous variables (temperature, NDVI) are converted into discrete classes for clear visualisation.

  4. 04

    Serve

    Each layer is registered as a Google Earth Engine XYZ tile endpoint.

  5. 05

    Render

    Tile URLs are consumed by a Leaflet WebGIS with toggleable layers and opacity control.

  6. 06

    Archive

    Every map is exported as high-resolution PNG and as GeoTIFF to Google Drive.

04

Key Findings

🔥

Fire dominates northern savannas

Over 90% of VIIRS detections in October 2023 occurred in the tropical savannas of the Northern Territory and Queensland — a region that burns annually during the late dry season.

🌡️

Deserts exceed 45 °C seasonally

Mean daytime LST in 2019 exceeded 45 °C across the Great Victoria and Gibson deserts, while coastal Tasmania and Victoria averaged below 20 °C.

🌿

Sharp coast-to-interior gradient

Mean NDVI for 2010–2021 exceeded 0.6 along the east coast and southwest forests, but stayed below 0.15 across the central arid zone.

🌲

Forest loss concentrated in the east

Between 2020 and 2022, measurable forest loss occurred primarily in NSW and southeast Queensland — coinciding with post-bushfire recovery and ongoing land-use change.

05

Reproducibility

All layers are generated from freely available datasets via the Google Earth Engine Python API. The complete pipeline lives in a Jupyter notebook plus three helper scripts.

  • Python 3.10+ with earthengine-api and geemap
  • Jupyter notebook for the analysis
  • Static HTML / CSS / JS WebGIS deployed on Vercel
  • All inputs open-licensed — NASA, USGS, University of Maryland
