01

Overview

This project builds a cloud-native geospatial pipeline that monitors eight environmental signals across the Australian continent using freely available satellite data processed on Google Earth Engine. It delivers an interactive WebGIS, eight static map products, a per-pixel vegetation trend analysis (2015–2024), and a fully reproducible Python workflow with rigorous QA/QC masking and Mann-Kendall-style statistical inference.

03

Processing Pipeline

  1. 01

    Ingest

    Seven Earth Engine ImageCollections are filtered by date and Australia's national boundary.

  2. 02

    QA & Mask

    Per-pixel QA bands (SummaryQA, QC_Day) remove clouds, shadows, and low-quality observations.

  3. 03

    Reduce

    Temporal aggregations — mean, max, count — produce one summary image per layer.

  4. 04

    Trend

    Per-pixel OLS slope over 2015–2024, classified into significant increase / decrease / no trend.

  5. 05

    Classify

    Continuous variables are converted into discrete classes for clear visualisation.

  6. 06

    Serve

    Each layer and the trend raster are registered as Google Earth Engine XYZ tile endpoints.

  7. 07

    Render

    Tile URLs are consumed by a Leaflet WebGIS with toggleable layers and opacity control.

  8. 08

    Archive

    Every map is exported as high-resolution PNG and as GeoTIFF to Google Drive.

04

Key Findings

🌿

Australia is greening at +0.002 NDVI/year

Mean per-pixel OLS slope over 2015–2024 is +0.002070 NDVI/year. Approximately 57% of the continent shows significant greening vs. 20% browning — consistent with continental-scale recovery following the 2019–2020 bushfire season.

🔥

Fire dominates northern savannas

Over 90% of VIIRS detections in October 2023 occurred in the tropical savannas of the Northern Territory and Queensland — a region that burns annually during the late dry season.

🌡️

Deserts exceed 45 °C seasonally

Mean daytime LST in 2019 exceeded 45 °C across the Great Victoria and Gibson deserts, while Tasmania and Victoria averaged below 20 °C.

🌲

Forest loss concentrated in the east

Between 2020 and 2022, measurable forest loss occurred primarily in NSW and southeast Queensland — coinciding with post-bushfire recovery and ongoing land-use change.

05

Reproducibility

All layers are generated from freely available datasets via the Google Earth Engine Python API. The complete pipeline lives in a Jupyter notebook plus a Python package (aem) that includes QA/QC masking, spectral indices, and trend analysis with 28 unit tests.

  • Python 3.10+ with earthengine-api, pymannkendall, rasterio, rioxarray
  • Jupyter notebook for exploratory analysis
  • aem package: masking, indices, trends, validation (18 passing tests)
  • Static HTML / CSS / JS WebGIS deployed on Vercel
  • All inputs open-licensed — NASA, USGS, University of Maryland
